“Whippet in bed” sound composition

A glitchy, low-bit soundtrack for a low-poly animation of a 3D dog being modelled, where the sound sharpens as the model comes into focus.

My role
Sound designer and composer
Year
2025
Tools
REAPER

The story behind it

I wanted the sound to do more than accompany the animation. The video builds up to the completion of a 3D model of a whippet in bed, so I made the sound build up to it too, with its clarity standing in for the clarity of the model. As the dog takes shape, the music moves from crunchy 4-bit sounds towards 16-bit.

The brief

For Assignment 1, I had to design and compose sound for an animation. I looked at Cafe figures first, for the variety of sounds it could suggest, but chose “Whippet in bed”, an animation by Gina Moore, because its simplicity gave me more freedom in the style of the sound design.

Research & concept

The muted colours and polygonal shapes gave the animation a Nintendo 64 feel, which gave me the idea of paying homage to that era. The way the model glitches, stutters and loads suggested sounds that were low-bit, distorted and glitchy.

I took advice on finding inspiration from sound editor Richard King, then studied films with an electronic-synth style. I focused on the Ex Machina (2014) tracks “Ana” and “Bunsen Burner”, and on how composers Geoff Barrow and Ben Salisbury use tone, pitch, volume, rhythm and silence to build tension and lead towards an important moment.

Sound design process

Following another of King’s recommendations, I began with broad strokes rather than aiming at a specific moment. I made sounds that could fit into many parts of the video, found a place for each, then altered and refined them to fit. That also showed me which gaps were left to fill.

I used rhythm and ambience to build tension slowly, distortion to suggest uncertainty, and silence as a contrast to the loud, fast sounds so that the tension lands harder. The whole piece builds to the moment the model is complete.

The key mirror comes eighteen seconds in, when the dog’s head is remodelled into a low-poly shape and the synth drops abruptly to 4-bit.

The animation has very little movement, which ruled out a diegetic approach based on sounds from the scene, so I stayed with the synth idea throughout.

Reflection

I hoped to be more experimental and abstract, but time constraints probably made me play it safer. Looking back, I’m still unsure whether the 4-bit drop at eighteen seconds makes the mirroring too obvious, and whether I over-processed parts of the composition.

The build-up kept attention through what is otherwise a conventional video, but treating the finished model as a pivotal moment could leave a viewer underwhelmed. I’m still fairly satisfied with the atmosphere I created while keeping the electronic-synth style.
